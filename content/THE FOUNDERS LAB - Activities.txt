1.  Idea Blender
Session 1 · Startup Ideation   ·   Spin the decks + swipe
Turns the scary blank-page moment of "think of a startup idea" into a playful slot-machine. Students mash random ingredients together, laugh at the weird combos, then sharpen the one that actually sparks something.
How to play
1.    Three decks sit on screen, a WHO deck (sleepy students, street vendors, busy moms…), a PROBLEM deck (food, boredom, money, transport…) and a TWIST deck (make it free, make it a game, add AI…).
2.    Tap SPIN, each deck lands on a random card, giving a fresh combo like "busy moms + boredom + make it a game".
3.    Swipe combos you like into a Shortlist; skip the ones that don't click. Spin as many times as you want.
4.    Pick your favourite from the shortlist and complete one sentence: "We will help ___ with ___ by ___."
What's to be done  →  3 shortlisted ideas + 1 refined idea sentence, the seed their Idea Canvas is built on.
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced students must justify why now (why this idea makes sense today) before locking it in.
 
2.  Mom Test Trainer
Session 2 · Validating & Refining   ·   Swipe (good vs leading)
Real customer interviews happen offline in the session, this game trains the skill beforehand: asking questions that get honest answers instead of polite lies.
How to play
1.    An interview question flashes up, e.g. "Would you use an app that helps you study?"
2.    Student swipes RIGHT for a good question or LEFT for a leading one.
3.    Instant feedback explains WHY, good questions ask about the past and real behaviour, leading ones fish for a yes.
4.    A score and streak build as they go; a short summary of the golden rules appears at the end.
What's to be done  →  A score + the 4 Mom Test rules internalised, ready for their live interviews in the session.
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced round: rewrite a leading question into a good one and the game checks the shape of the answer.
 
3.  Empathy Map Builder
Session 3 · Design Thinking   ·   Drag-and-drop canvas
Gets students inside their customer's head by sorting real observations into how the person speaks, thinks, acts and feels.
How to play
1.    A blank canvas shows four quadrants: SAY, THINK, DO, FEEL.
2.    A bunch of sticky notes (things a real user said or did) sits at the bottom, students drag each into the right quadrant.
3.    They can also add their own notes by typing onto a blank sticky.
4.    When all four quadrants have notes, the map is complete and can be saved.
What's to be done  →  A completed empathy map for their target customer.
 
4.  Find Your Customer
Session 4 · Decode the Market   ·   Swipe (right = target)
Teaches that a startup is NOT for everyone. Students learn to name exactly who their customer is by rejecting who it isn't.
How to play
1.    Person-cards appear one at a time , "14-yr-old gamer", "tired mom of two", "hostel student", etc.
2.    Swipe RIGHT if this person has the problem their startup solves, LEFT if they don't.
3.    Every RIGHT collects into a growing "My Customers" panel.
4.    At the end they review the list and pick their #1 ideal customer.
What's to be done  →  A shortlist of target customers + one chosen ideal customer.
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced students add a one-line reason each person qualifies (or add a willingness-to-pay).
 
5.  Who's Who Sort
Session 5 · Startup Ecosystem   ·   Tap-to-bucket
Introduces the five players every startup lives among, without a complicated diagram. Fast, scored, and repeatable.
How to play
1.    Real-world examples drop in one by one: a bank, a delivery rider, the local shopkeeper, a government office, your buyer.
2.    Student taps which of the 5 roles it belongs to: Investor · Supplier · Partner · Regulator · Customer.
3.    Correct taps build a score and streak; wrong taps show the right answer with a one-line why.
4.    After the round, they list the real people/organisations in their OWN startup's ecosystem.
What's to be done  →  The 5 ecosystem roles learned + their own ecosystem listed.
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced students name a real company for each role in their sector as well.
 
6.  MVP Slicer
Session 6 · Product-Market Fit   ·   Drag  but only 3 slots
Teaches ruthless prioritisation. The lesson is the constraint: you literally cannot fit every feature, so you must choose.
How to play
1.    A pile of feature cards sits in the middle (e.g. login, chat, rewards, dark mode, notifications).
2.    Two columns appear: MVP (only 3 slots) and LATER (unlimited).
3.    Students drag features across  but the MVP column refuses a 4th card.
4.    They must swap and rethink until only the 3 most essential features remain in MVP.
What's to be done  →  Their 3 must-have MVP features + everything else parked in "Later".
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced students write one line on why each of the 3 is non-negotiable.
 
7.  Face of the Brand
Session 7 · Digital & Social Media Marketing   ·   Tap / type quiz
Teaches founder-led marketing, the idea that people follow PEOPLE, not faceless logos. Every answer in the game is a human being, so the lesson lands on its own.
How to play
1.    A founder or creator's name appears, the student types (or picks) the brand that person built.
2.    Instant right/wrong feedback, score and streak, using Indian creator and founder-led brands teens recognise.
3.    Halfway through, a one-line insight surfaces: notice how every one of these brands has a human face people actually follow.
4.    Final beat, the personal turn: "Who would be the face of YOUR startup, and why?" answered in one line.
What's to be done  →  A set of correct guesses + a one-line decision on what type of a front facing “face or identity” do the funders want to create for their own startup.
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced students sketch a 4-post launch marketing ideas for their chosen founder-face.


8.  Competitor 2×2 Plotter
Session 8 · Know Your Competition   ·   Drag-to-plot on a grid
Turns competitor analysis into a spatial hunt for the empty space on the map, the gap where their startup can win.
How to play
1.    A grid appears with two axes, e.g. Price (low→high) and Quality (low→high).
2.    Students drag competitor dots onto the grid based on where each one sits.
3.    As dots fill in, crowded and empty zones become obvious.
4.    They drop their OWN startup into the emptiest, most valuable space.
What's to be done  →  A competitor map + the identified market gap their startup will own.
 
9.  Price It Right
Session 9 · Financials   ·   Single slider + live numbers
Makes money intuitive without any jargon. One product, one slider, and the whole price-vs-profit trade-off becomes a game of finding the sweet spot.
How to play
1.    One product on screen (say, a handmade friendship bracelet) with one control: a PRICE slider.
2.    Slide it and two numbers update live — profit per bracelet, and how many people will buy at that price.
3.    Price too high → nobody buys. Too low → no profit. A "total profit" meter reacts in real time.
4.    Students hunt for the price that maxes total profit,  that's their score.
What's to be done  →  Their chosen price + profit-per-unit figured out through play, no CAC/LTV needed for Class 8.
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced (Class 11–12) unlock a fixed-costs layer that reveals the break-even point.
 
10.  Pitch Deck Sequencer
Session 10 · Pitch & Shark Tank   ·   Reorder the slides
Teaches the storytelling spine of a great pitch,  that order matters before students ever build the real deck.
How to play
1.    The 10 slide types appear shuffled: Problem, Solution, Market, Product, Traction, Business Model, Competition, Team, The Ask, Vision.
2.    Students drag them into the order that tells the most convincing story.
3.    The game confirms the strong sequence and explains why it flows that way.
4.    For each slide they type one line about their own startup.
What's to be done  →  A correctly ordered 10-slide skeleton, pre-filled with one line each — feeding straight into the pitch-deck chatbot.
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced students must open with a hook line and close with a clear ask.
 
11.  Founder Level Up
Session 11 · Bespoke Mentoring   ·   Tap-quiz + badge reveal
A motivating self-check before the Makers Mela finale, a badge reveal, not a boring to-do list.
How to play
1.    5 quick questions, each answered with a tap: "Can you say your problem in one line?" → Yes / Sort of / Not yet.
2.    Each answer fills a power bar a little more.
3.    At the end a badge is revealed: Rookie → Rising Founder → Mela Ready.
4.    One nudge appears on the single thing to fix before the finale.
What's to be done  →  A shareable Founder Level badge + one clear next step before Makers Mela.
Advanced (Class 11–12):  Advanced students get a tougher rubric and a stretch goal to reach the top badge.
